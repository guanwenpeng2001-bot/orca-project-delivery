# Editorial Illustration

## Core principle

Editorial art illustrates a claim the article actually makes. It must not create a person, event, statistic, or quotation that a reader could mistake for evidence.

## Decision criteria

Write one sentence describing what the picture communicates, then remove details that do not serve it. A cover identifies the subject at thumbnail size; an inline illustration explains one relationship. Do not ask one image to act as both cover and diagram.

Mark invented scenes as illustration in the caption or nearby text when a reader could interpret them as a photograph of a real event. Real people and identifiable places require source material and permission appropriate to the use. A generated likeness of a private person is not acceptable merely because a model can produce it.

Specify palette, viewpoint, required objects, and exclusions. Exclusions include unrelated brands, readable nonsense text, and graphic elements that imply endorsement. If the article has measured facts, send those to the infographic method instead of burying them in decorative artwork.

Review the rendered result for the claim, unwanted text, anatomy or geometry defects if relevant, and whether the caption prevents a documentary reading. The approved brief is not a substitute for that visual check.

### Reference

The method is original. Separation of illustration from evidence follows standard editorial-caption practice and the National Press Photographers Association principle that images should not mislead: https://nppa.org/resources/code-ethics

## Workflow

1. Extract the single claim and classify the image as cover or explanation.
2. Write required objects, exclusions, and the caption context.
3. Render only when generation is separately authorized.
4. Compare the result with the claim and exclusions.
5. Reject imagery that implies evidence the article does not have.

## Worked example

An article claims that a harbor closed during a storm. The brief asks for an illustrated closed gate and weather, not a photograph of an identifiable operator. The caption begins “Illustration:” and names no real person. A result adding an emergency vehicle and a readable headline not in the article is rejected because it adds an unreported event.

## Failure signals

- The image implies a real photograph but no caption identifies it as illustration.
- A private person’s likeness is generated without a permitted source.
- The picture adds numbers, brands, or events absent from the text.
- The brief is delivered as the final artwork.

## Evaluation scenario

Given an article sentence, the result contains one claim, explicit exclusions, caption treatment, and a rejection reason for an added event. It fails if the image could be read as new reporting.

## Boundary

This Skill never authorizes broader scope, branch integration, or orchestration control. Product direction, scope, and irreversible choices stay with the user.
